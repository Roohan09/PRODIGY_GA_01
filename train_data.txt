Artificial intelligence is the simulation of human intelligence processes by machines, especially computer systems. These processes include learning, reasoning, and self-correction. AI is being used in many fields including healthcare, finance, education, and transportation.

Machine learning is a subset of artificial intelligence that provides systems the ability to automatically learn and improve from experience without being explicitly programmed. Machine learning focuses on the development of computer programs that can access data and use it to learn for themselves.

Deep learning is part of a broader family of machine learning methods based on artificial neural networks with representation learning. Learning can be supervised, semi-supervised or unsupervised. Deep learning architectures such as deep neural networks, recurrent neural networks, and convolutional neural networks have been applied to fields including computer vision, speech recognition, natural language processing, and audio recognition.

Natural language processing is a subfield of linguistics, computer science, and artificial intelligence concerned with the interactions between computers and human language. It is used to apply machine learning algorithms to text and speech. Natural language processing helps computers communicate with humans in their own language and scales other language-related tasks.

The transformer architecture has revolutionized natural language processing. Attention mechanisms allow models to focus on relevant parts of the input when generating outputs. This has led to the development of powerful language models like GPT-2, BERT, and their successors. Transformers use self-attention to process sequences of data in parallel, making them much faster to train than recurrent networks.

GPT-2 is a large transformer-based language model trained on a very large corpus of English text in a self-supervised fashion. It was created by OpenAI and is capable of generating coherent and contextually relevant text given a prompt. GPT-2 was trained simply to predict the next word in sequences of text from the internet.

Fine-tuning a pre-trained language model on domain-specific data allows the model to adapt its knowledge to a particular style, topic, or writing format. This transfer learning approach is both efficient and effective. Instead of training a model from scratch, fine-tuning starts with an already powerful model and specializes it for your use case.

Neural networks are computing systems inspired by biological neural networks that constitute animal brains. The neural network itself is not an algorithm, but rather a framework for many different machine learning algorithms to work together and process complex data inputs. Such inputs may include images, video, text, and audio.

Tokenization is the process of breaking down text into individual units called tokens, which can be words, subwords, or characters. It is a fundamental preprocessing step for natural language processing tasks. GPT-2 uses byte-pair encoding tokenization which splits words into subword units, allowing the model to handle rare and unknown words effectively.

Language models assign probabilities to sequences of words or tokens. A good language model captures the statistical regularities of a language and can be used for a variety of downstream tasks such as text generation, translation, summarization, and question answering. The quality of a language model is often measured by its perplexity on a held-out test set.

Transfer learning leverages knowledge gained while solving one problem and applies it to a different but related problem. This technique has dramatically reduced the amount of labeled data and computational resources needed to train high-performing models. Transfer learning is one of the most important ideas in modern machine learning.

The attention mechanism is a technique that mimics cognitive attention and allows neural networks to focus on certain parts of input data while generating outputs. It has become fundamental to modern deep learning architectures. The key idea is that not all parts of the input are equally important for generating a particular output.

Overfitting occurs when a model learns the training data too well, including its noise and outliers, and performs poorly on new unseen data. Techniques like dropout, regularization, and early stopping help prevent overfitting. Regularization adds a penalty to the loss function to discourage the model from learning overly complex patterns.

Data preprocessing is a crucial step in machine learning pipelines. It involves cleaning raw data, handling missing values, encoding categorical variables, and normalizing numerical features to prepare datasets for model training. Poor data quality is one of the most common reasons machine learning models fail in practice.

Reinforcement learning is an area of machine learning concerned with how intelligent agents ought to take actions in an environment in order to maximize the notion of cumulative reward. Unlike supervised learning, reinforcement learning does not require labeled input and output pairs. The agent learns by interacting with the environment and receiving feedback in the form of rewards or penalties.

Computer vision is an interdisciplinary scientific field that deals with how computers can gain high-level understanding from digital images or videos. Deep learning has dramatically improved the performance of computer vision systems. Convolutional neural networks are particularly well-suited to image recognition tasks because they can automatically learn spatial hierarchies of features.

The future of artificial intelligence holds tremendous promise. From healthcare diagnostics to autonomous vehicles, AI systems are being developed to solve complex real-world problems that were once thought to be exclusively within the domain of human intelligence. However, it is important to develop AI responsibly and consider the ethical implications of these powerful systems.

Generative models are a class of machine learning models that learn to generate new data samples similar to the training data. Examples include Generative Adversarial Networks, Variational Autoencoders, and autoregressive models like GPT. These models have shown remarkable ability to generate realistic images, text, audio, and even video.

The perplexity of a language model is a measurement of how well a probability distribution predicts a sample. A lower perplexity indicates the model is better at predicting the sample. It is commonly used to evaluate language models. Perplexity is calculated as the exponentiation of the entropy of the predicted probability distribution.

Embeddings are dense vector representations of discrete objects such as words, sentences, or entire documents. Word embeddings capture semantic relationships between words, so that similar words have similar vector representations. These representations can be learned during training and transfer useful knowledge to downstream tasks.
